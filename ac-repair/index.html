When to Call for AC Repair in West Des Moines

Spotting the early warning signs lets you act before the problem gets expensive. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • An AC that turns on and off every few minutes but never cools down the house
  • A hiss or gurgle from the lines, a common sign of leaking refrigerant
  • An iced-over evaporator coil or frozen refrigerant lines
  • Breaker trips or a burning odor at startup
  • Rising electric costs paired with a sticky, uncomfortable house
  • Vents blowing air that is barely cooler than the room
  • Puddles near the air handler or water stains on the ceiling beneath it
  • The outdoor unit humming or clicking but the fan not spinning

Why It Matters

Why Professional AC Repair Matters

An air conditioner puts several hazards into one cabinet. The capacitor can deliver a painful shock long after the breaker is off, the condensing unit is wired for 240 volts, and the refrigerant circuit is a sealed, pressurized loop that federal rules forbid venting. Servicing it properly takes gauges, a recovery machine, and a vacuum pump, not a DIY recharge can. Diagnosis has its own traps. A frozen coil is as likely to be an airflow issue as a charge issue, and recharging a system that still leaks wastes money while moisture and acid work on the compressor.

Professional service sees the full picture, not just the symptom. Our technicians meter electrical parts before replacing them, make charge decisions from measured superheat and subcooling, locate leaks with electronic detectors and dye, and check the filter, blower, and static pressure any time airflow could be the issue. The result is a repair that lasts the season instead of a guess that fails in three weeks. If your system is not worth saving, you will hear it from us directly, backed by numbers rather than a sales pitch.

Maintenance

Maintenance Tips for AC Repair

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Schedule a spring tune-up so weak parts are caught before summer arrives
  • Keep supply and return vents open; closing rooms raises static pressure on the blower
  • Shade helps, but never enclose the outdoor unit; it needs free airflow on all sides
  • Clear the condensate drain when you first turn on the AC each year
  • Swap filters monthly while the AC is running hard to keep the evaporator coil from icing
  • Pay attention to buzzing or clicking when the AC starts, a common warning of a failing capacitor
  • Rinse the outdoor condenser coil each spring and keep vegetation two feet back
