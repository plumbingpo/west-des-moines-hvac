Keeping Your Des Moines HVAC System Running Longer

Routine maintenance is the difference between a system that lasts and one that breaks down in the middle of the season. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: fresh filters every one to three months, two feet of clearance around the outdoor condenser, a gentle rinse each spring for the condenser coil, and a professional tune-up every spring and fall, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners last 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and showing symptoms, such as climbing utility bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or repairs stacking up season after season, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one in the middle of a cold snap.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but left alone for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a few miserable nights.

Comfort Challenges Des Moines Homeowners Face

Most local HVAC failures give warning signs before they happen. A breaker that trips every few days, a condenser that hums louder each month, rooms drifting further from the thermostat setting, a musty smell at startup. These are the early symptoms of failing capacitors, refrigerant leaks, dirty coils, and duct problems, and caught early they're an affordable repair. Ignored, they take out compressors and heat exchangers, the two most expensive parts in the system. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
