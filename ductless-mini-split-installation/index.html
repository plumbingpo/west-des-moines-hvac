Ductless Mini Split Installation, Done Properly

Design is where mini split projects succeed or fail, and West Des Moines HVAC does it deliberately. Each zone gets sized to its actual load, because a head too big for the room cools fast but leaves the air clammy. Multi-zone systems bring their own engineering: the outdoor unit's capacity has to be matched sensibly across the connected heads, line set lengths and elevation changes must stay inside the manufacturer's tables, and the choice between wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor units affects both comfort and how the room looks. Our experienced technicians walk every room with you, place heads where the physics and the sightlines agree, and route line sets cleanly, in finished line-hide outdoors.

Our installation process is held to the same standard as our biggest central systems. Nitrogen goes into the line set first to prove it holds pressure, then a vacuum pump pulls it down to a level verified on a micron gauge. Flares are torqued and leak-tested one by one, since that joint is where sloppy work shows up a season later. Drainage is set up with real fall or a reliable condensate pump, and the refrigerant charge is adjusted to match the line length. Finally, every zone is commissioned in both modes and we show you how the remote actually works. Why It Matters

Why Professional Ductless Mini Split Installation Matters

Because a mini split looks like a simple box on a wall, a lot of them get installed like one, and they fail in predictable ways. The weakest link is the flare joint. Form one badly or leave it under-torqued and it seeps refrigerant slowly, so nobody notices until the head is blowing room-temperature air months later. Skip the nitrogen test and the measured vacuum, and moisture stays in a circuit that inverter compressors do not forgive. Push the line set past the manufacturer's length or lift limits and you lose capacity without a single alarm. Forget the drain slope and the first muggy week leaves water marks on the drywall.

Sizing discipline matters even more here than with central systems, because the smallest available heads are already generous for many bedrooms; the wrong pick short-cycles, leaves humidity behind, and never settles. Our experienced technicians size each zone honestly, torque and leak-test every flare, pressure-test and evacuate to a measured vacuum, weigh charge adjustments to the line length, and commission each head in both modes. Refrigerant work is federally regulated, and the electrical side needs a properly sized circuit and disconnect. The result is what a mini split should be: silent, efficient, and uneventful for fifteen years, instead of a mysterious underperformer from a rushed weekend install.

Homeowner Tips

How to Prevent Ductless Mini Split Installation Problems

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Have the indoor blower wheel and coil professionally cleaned before they grow anything
  • Resist turning the system off and on; an inverter is most efficient holding one steady temperature
  • Get a yearly professional checkup of charge, flare joints, and drainage while any fix is still small
  • Brief defrost cycles and a little steam in winter are normal, but a unit encased in ice needs service
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of snow, leaves, and grass clippings in every season
  • Pull and rinse the indoor filter screens monthly, because they fill up much faster than you would expect
  • On humid summer days, confirm the drain line outside is dripping as it should
